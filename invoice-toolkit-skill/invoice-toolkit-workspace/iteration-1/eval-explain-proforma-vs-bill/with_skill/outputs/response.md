**Proforma invoice vs. normal (standard) invoice**

The difference is about *when* it's sent and *what it obligates*, not the content:

- A **proforma invoice** is sent *before* delivery. It's a preview/estimate of what the customer will owe, so they can plan for the payment — it is not a demand for payment yet.
- A **standard invoice** is sent once the goods/services have actually been delivered (or delivery is imminent), and it *is* a real demand for payment by the due date.

Since you said you haven't shipped anything yet: a proforma invoice is almost certainly what you want to send now. Sending a normal invoice pre-delivery is the classic mistake here — it implies you're billing for completed work when you're not.

**Is a "bill" just another word for invoice?**

Not quite — they're functionally similar (both list what's owed), but a bill implies faster, often immediate payment (think a restaurant bill, paid on the spot), while an invoice usually carries longer, defined payment terms (e.g. net 30). So "bill" and "invoice" get used loosely as synonyms in casual speech, but if you're being precise, use "invoice" for B2B work with payment terms — which is what you're doing with a client.

For reference, the other documents in this transaction typically go: Purchase Order (buyer requests) → Invoice (seller bills) → Receipt (proof payment was made). A receipt is specifically proof of payment *already made*, so that's not relevant yet either.

**Bottom line for your situation:** send a proforma invoice now, since nothing has shipped. Once you deliver, follow up with the standard invoice that actually starts the payment clock.
