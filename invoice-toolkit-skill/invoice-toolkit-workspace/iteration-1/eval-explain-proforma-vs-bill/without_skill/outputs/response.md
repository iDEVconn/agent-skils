Good question to sort out before you send anything - here's the short version:

**Proforma invoice vs. (real) invoice**

- A **proforma invoice** is a preview/quote, not a request for payment. You send it *before* the work is finished or goods are shipped, so the client knows exactly what they'll owe (amount, line items, taxes, terms) and can approve it, arrange funding, or clear customs. It's not legally binding as a payment demand, it doesn't create an accounts-receivable entry in your books, and it usually shouldn't use the same sequential invoice numbering as your real invoices (many people label it clearly as "Proforma Invoice" and add a note like "This is not a demand for payment").
- A **normal (commercial) invoice** is the real deal - sent *after* delivery/completion (or at the agreed billing point), it's a legally binding request for payment, gets a proper sequential invoice number, is recorded in your accounting/tax records, and starts the clock on your payment terms (e.g., due in 30 days).

Since you haven't shipped anything yet, what you want right now is a **proforma invoice**. Once the work/goods are actually delivered, follow up with the real invoice referencing it.

**Is "bill" just another word for invoice?**

Mostly yes, in practice - there's no strict legal distinction, and in casual/small-business conversation people use them interchangeably. If there's a nuance, it's about perspective and tone:

- "Invoice" is the term the *seller/business* uses, especially B2B, and usually implies payment terms (net 30, etc.).
- "Bill" is more often used from the *buyer's/receiver's* side, or in consumer-facing/immediate-payment contexts (a restaurant bill, a utility bill) where payment is expected right away rather than on terms.

For a B2B client relationship like yours, "invoice" is the more professional, standard term - I'd stick with "proforma invoice" now and "invoice" later, and avoid "bill" in the document titles.
